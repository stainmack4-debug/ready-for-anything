# Official FUNAAB Web Properties: Courses, Synopses, Guides, Past Questions, and PDFs

**Research scope.** This inventory covers publicly accessible material discovered from the Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta (FUNAAB) web ecosystem: `funaab.edu.ng`, `admission.funaab.edu.ng`, `pg.unaab.edu.ng`, `pgs.funaab.edu.ng`, selected departmental/college pages and PDF directories. It does not use authenticated areas, payment pages, paywalls, robots-rule workarounds, or other access-control bypasses. URLs are deduplicated. The independent **FUNAAB 101** student guide is reported separately and is not treated as a university-controlled source.

## Executive findings

The highest-value official source is FUNAAB’s OpenCourseWare and courseware collection. The university explicitly describes OpenCourseWare as freely shared academic material containing course descriptions, requirements, reading lists, lecture notes, and related documents.[1] The linked material includes undergraduate course lists, course synopses, lecture notes, and some downloadable PDFs. The admission portal supplies current programme names, minimum acceptable UTME scores, and a current admission-requirements PDF.[2] The postgraduate ecosystem contains programme-duration information, an official postgraduate advert PDF, and detailed departmental course outlines and synopses, especially on the Statistics department page.[3] Official past-question pages exist, but several are sparse landing pages; direct past-question PDFs are more reliably discoverable in the university’s older `wp-content/uploads` directory.[4]

FUNAAB 101 is useful for navigation and student-oriented organization, but it expressly identifies itself as an independent resource and directs users to verify fees, deadlines, and official decisions with the university.[5] It is therefore ranked below FUNAAB-controlled pages and PDFs.

## Official university pages and discovered materials

### Main FUNAAB site and OpenCourseWare

The official OpenCourseWare landing page says that FUNAAB materials include **course descriptions, course requirements, reading lists, lecture notes, and other documents**. It organizes discovery by college, department, and course code. The colleges named there are Agricultural Management and Rural Development, Animal Science and Livestock Production, Engineering, Environmental Resources Management, Food Science and Human Ecology, Natural Sciences, Plant Science and Crop Production, and Veterinary Medicine.[1]

The official courseware category exposes course titles and synopses. Representative entries include ABG 301 Principles of Genetics, ABG 401 Heritable and Non-Heritable Traits, AGE 401 Design of Agricultural & Food Processing Machines I, AGE 403 Hydraulics Engineering, AGE 407 Engineering Properties and Processing of Agricultural Materials, AGE 409 Introduction to Agricultural Structures Designs, AGE 502 Farm Machinery II, AGE 503 Farm Power I, FST 509 Food Packaging, FWM 303 Natural Ecosystems, HSM 200 Introduction to Home Science Management, HSM 201 Basic Design, and HSM 202 Introduction to Textile and Fabric Design.[6]

The Agricultural Engineering course page lists AGE 102 Introduction to Agricultural Engineering; AGE 201 Technical Drawing I; AGE 202 Engineering Drawing II; AGE 204 Workshop Practice; AGE 222 Introduction to Farm Machine; AGE 301 Farm Mechanics; AGE 302 Design of Agricultural Machine Elements; AGE 303 Agricultural Land Surveying; AGE 304 Statistics for Engineers; AGE 322 Agricultural Mechanization; AGE 403 Hydraulics Engineering; AGE 405 Farm Electrification; AGE 407 Engineering Properties and Processing of Agricultural Materials; AGE 409 Introduction to Agricultural Structures Designs; AGE 501 Farm Machinery I; AGE 502 Farm Machinery II; AGE 503 Farm Power I; AGE 504 Farm Power II; AGE 505 Soil and Water Conservation; AGE 506 Irrigation Engineering; AGE 507 Farm Structure and Environmental Control; AGE 512 Food and Crop Storage Technology; and AGE 514 Water Resources Development.[7]

### Departmental course synopses

The official Physics synopsis provides extensive descriptions. PHS 101 General Physics covers units and dimensions, mechanics, gravitation, oscillation, work and energy, elasticity, hydrostatics and hydrodynamics, temperature, heat, and introductory thermodynamics. PHS 102 General Physics II covers electric charge and fields, potential, direct current, magnetic and thermal effects, physical optics, radiation, photons, atomic theory, and radioactivity. PHS 105 and PHS 106 adapt physics to agriculture and biological students. Higher-level entries include PHS 231 Waves and Optics, PHS 242 Electricity, PHS 251 Introductory Modern Physics, PHS 341 Electromagnetism, PHS 344 Electronics, PHS 352 Quantum Physics, PHS 354 Introductory Nuclear Physics, PHS 355 Biophysics I, PHS 473 Computational Physics, and PHS 482 Ionospheric Physics.[8]

The Forestry and Wildlife synopsis includes FWM 201 Introduction to Forest and Wildlife Resources Management, FWM 203 Principles of Conservation in Tropical Africa, FWM 204 Principles of Range Ecology and Management, FWM 301 Principles of Silviculture, FWM 303 Natural Ecosystems, FWM 305 Introduction to Forest and Wildlife Management, FWM 307 Introduction to Forest and Wildlife Extension, FWM 309 Wood Anatomy, Formation and Properties, FWM 313 Forest Resources Inventory and Mensuration, FWM 306 Wildlife Ecology and Management, FWM 314 Principles of Agroforestry, FWM 316 Seed and Forest Nursery Technology, FWM 318 Wildlife Population Analysis, FWM 401 Forestry Inventory and Management, FWM 403 Silviculture Techniques, FWM 411 Harvesting, Processing and Wood Utilization, FWM 415 Zoo and Parks Management Techniques, and FWM 419 Ecological Survey.[9]

The Electrical/Electronics synopsis covers ELE 201 Applied Electricity I, ELE 202 Applied Electricity II, ELE 301 and ELE 302 Electric Circuit Theory, ELE 303 and ELE 304 Electromagnetic Theory, ELE 305 and ELE 306 Electrical Machines, ELE 307 and ELE 308 Electronic Circuits, ELE 309 Physical Electronics, ELE 401 Electric Power Principles, ELE 403 Servomechanism and Control, and ELE 405 Communication Principles. Topics include circuit laws, network theorems, semiconductors, AC systems, transformers, motors and generators, digital logic, control systems, power stations, modulation, transmission media, sampling, and pulse-code modulation.[10]

The Mathematics synopsis states that MTS 101 Algebra covers set theory, real numbers, indices, logarithms, surds, polynomials, equations, sequences and series, binomial theorem, matrices, determinants, complex numbers, Argand diagrams, De Moivre’s theorem, and roots of unity. MTS 102 covers calculus and trigonometry; MTS 103 covers vectors and geometry; MTS 104 covers mechanics; and MTS 105 adapts algebra and trigonometry for biological sciences and agriculture.[11]

Other discovered official synopsis pages include Agricultural Administration, Home Science and Management, Animal Physiology, Animal Nutrition, and a Biology/Pure and Applied Zoology synopsis. The search-discovered descriptions include MTS 101, HSM course content, animal physiology, and ANN 202 Introduction to Animal Biochemistry. These pages should be checked individually for currentness because many are legacy WordPress pages.

Examples include Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Microbiology, Public Health, Botany, Zoology, Science Laboratory Technology, Computer Science, Cyber Security, Data Science, Information and Communication Technology, Information Systems, Information Technology, Software Engineering, Food Science and Technology, Nutrition and Dietetics, Chemistry, Geophysics, Mathematics, Physics, Statistics, Agricultural Engineering, Civil Engineering, Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Mechatronic Engineering, Veterinary Medicine, Accounting, Banking and Finance, Business Administration, Economics, Entrepreneurial Studies, and Library and Information Science.[12]

### Postgraduate pages and departmental subdomains

The official PG portal states that the Post-Graduate School offers Postgraduate Diplomas, Masters, and Doctor of Philosophy programmes. It gives published duration ranges: PhD full-time six semesters minimum and ten maximum; Masters full-time four minimum and eight maximum; Professional Masters full-time three minimum and six maximum; and PGD full-time two minimum and four maximum, with separate part-time ranges.[13]

The PG portal links an official 2025/2026 advertisement PDF. The official postgraduate subdomain also exposes CEADESE specialized programmes, including Agricultural Economics and Environmental Policy, Agricultural Mechanization, Crop and Pasture Production, Environmental Systems and Climate Change, and Food-related programmes.[14]

The official Statistics department page is unusually rich. Its postgraduate diploma outlines include STS 701 Probability Theory, STS 703 Design and Analysis of Experiments, STS 705 Econometric Methods, STS 709 Stochastic Processes, STS 711 Time Series Analysis, STS 713 Distribution Theory, STS 702 Statistical Inference, STS 704 Sampling Theory and Methods, STS 708 Multivariate Analysis, STS 712 Statistical Computing, STS 799 Project, and electives such as Operations Research, Applied Regression Analysis, Theory of Games, Statistical Quality Control, Biometric Methods, Demography, Decision Theory, and Non-parametric Methods.[15]

The Statistics synopsis describes STS 701 as probability spaces, conditional probability, independence, Bayes’ theorem, random variables, distributions, moments, generating functions, limiting distributions, and elementary stochastic processes. STS 702 covers estimation, hypothesis testing, regression, analysis of variance, and multivariate analysis. STS 703 covers complete and incomplete block designs, Latin squares, factorial experiments, confounding, fractional replication, and response-surface design. STS 705 covers linear, nonlinear, simultaneous-equation, and time-series economic models. STS 708 covers multivariate normal distributions, principal components, discriminant, factor, and cluster analysis. STS 710 covers inventory, scheduling, queuing, replacement, critical path, dynamic programming, simulation, games, and decision theory.[15]

## Academic guides, lecture notes, and study resources

Official lecture-note landing pages include Mathematics Lecture Notes and Physics Lecture Notes. The Mathematics page names MTS 421, MTS 103, MTS 103 B, MTS 441, MTS 105, MTS 101 A/B/C/D/F, MTS 211 A/B, MTS 311, MTS 411, MTS 363, MTS 105 NOTE, MTS 323, MTS 423, and MTS 461.[16] The Physics page similarly lists PHS 411, PHS 471, PHS 460 A/B, PHS 473, PHS 102, PHS 354, PHS 342, PHS 312, PHS 211, PHS 105, and PHS 101.[17]

OpenCourseWare PDF directories include lecture guides such as **CHM 101 Part B: Thermochemistry**, **GNS 203 Use of Library**, **MTS 101 Algebra**, **Soil and Plant Analysis**, and **Controlled Breeding of Cattle, Sheep and Goats**. These are direct public PDFs, but many are old and should be treated as archival study material rather than current curriculum documents.[18]

Current and historical academic-calendar PDFs were discovered in the university PDF directories. The current example is the **2025/2026 Academic Calendar**; older examples include the 2018/2019 and 2020/2021 calendars. Calendar dates must be interpreted by session and publication date.[19]

## Past-question resources

FUNAAB has official past-question landing pages for Computer Science, Nutrition and Dietetics, Food Science and Technology, EMT, MCE, Mathematics, and Electrical/Electronics Engineering. Some pages contain little extracted text or appear to rely on embedded page elements, so the existence of a landing page does not guarantee that a downloadable question file is currently linked.[20]

Direct public PDFs discovered through official FUNAAB directories include ANP 201 Past Questions 2006, ANP 502 Examination 2007/2008, ANP 502 Sample Examination, and a 500-level Food Science and Technology past-question PDF. These are valuable revision artifacts but are clearly dated. They should not be represented as current examination papers or as evidence of current assessment practice.[21]

## Independent FUNAAB 101 guide — separate label

**FUNAAB 101 is not an official university property.** Its homepage describes it as an independent student resource created to make FUNAAB information easier to find and understand. It organizes freshers’ information, registration, academic calendars, departments, student groups, course materials, past questions, official portals, and campus guidance. It explicitly says that important decisions such as fees, deadlines, and official decisions should be verified with the university.[5]

Because the guide is independent, it is useful as a discovery and orientation layer but should not outrank the university’s admission portal, official PDF documents, postgraduate pages, or departmental pages. No FUNAAB 101 page was used as the sole authority for any course synopsis, admission requirement, or official deadline in this report.

## Caveats and quality notes

1. The site group contains both current pages and legacy pages. Courseware and many PDFs appear to originate from earlier sessions, so publication age is material.
2. A page listing a course or programme is not necessarily a current curriculum or current admission rule. Current-session claims are limited to pages explicitly labeled 2026/2027 or 2025/2026.
3. Some PDF extraction responses exposed encoded PDF data rather than readable text. The direct URLs remain publicly discoverable, but the report does not infer details that were not visible in readable source text.
4. Some official past-question landing pages were sparse or appeared to depend on embedded links. The report distinguishes landing pages from direct downloadable PDFs.
5. The report does not treat third-party mirrors, Scribd, social-media posts, or unaffiliated sites as official FUNAAB sources.
6. FUNAAB 101 is explicitly independent and is ranked below official university sources.
7. No login, payment, paywall, robots restriction, or access control was bypassed.
