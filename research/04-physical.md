# FUNAAB Academic Domain: Physical sciences

**Scope.** This catalogue entry covers the six programmes shown by FUNAAB’s official 2026/2027 pre-admission screening page under **Physical Science**: Chemistry, Geophysics, Industrial Chemistry, Mathematics, Physics, and Statistics [1]. The report prioritizes official FUNAAB pages and official admission documents. It distinguishes programme-level statements from course-unit materials and does not infer a curriculum where FUNAAB has not published one.

## Executive finding

FUNAAB’s College of Physical Sciences (COLPHYS) page describes a four-year, NUC-accredited B.Sc. framework and identifies Chemistry, Computer Science, Mathematics, Physics, and Statistics as its departments [2]. The current official admission list nevertheless includes **Geophysics** and **Industrial Chemistry** in the Physical Science group [1]. This creates a source-consistency issue: Geophysics is not named among the five COLPHYS departments on the college page, while Industrial Chemistry is not presented there as a separate department. The safest tutor-catalogue treatment is therefore to list both programmes because FUNAAB’s admission system lists them, while explicitly flagging that the publicly available college/department pages do not provide a standalone current undergraduate programme description for either.

The official departmental pages give usable programme-level synopses for Chemistry, Mathematics, Physics, and Statistics. They discuss aims, graduate capabilities, broad applications, duration or graduation rules, and—in Chemistry—the six-month industrial attachment. They do **not** constitute a complete course-by-course syllabus. Public FUNAAB PDF material located in this search is mainly admission documentation and a postgraduate prospectus; no current official undergraduate handbook or complete curriculum PDF was found for these six programmes.

## Source-by-source notes and limits

### Chemistry

The official Chemistry department page says the department offers B.Sc., M.Sc., and Ph.D. programmes and that the B.Sc. courses are designed for a four-year degree. It frames the undergraduate programme as balanced training in chemistry theory, principles, applications, and practical skills, with intended destinations in pure chemistry research, tertiary teaching, industry, and self-employment [3]. The same page specifies a six-month industrial attachment in the third year [3]. These are **programme-level** claims. The page does not provide a complete semester-by-semester course-unit syllabus in the material reviewed.

### Geophysics

The official admission page lists Geophysics with a minimum acceptable UTME score of 180 [1]. The College of Physical Sciences page lists five departments but does not list Geophysics as a department [2]. FUNAAB’s official postgraduate-programmes page includes “Solid Earth Physics/Geophysics” as an M.Sc./Ph.D. field [4]. The official Geology page describes Geology, geological resources, and geo-environmental problems, but it is not a Geophysics programme page and should not be used to invent Geophysics course content [5].

### Industrial Chemistry

The official admission page lists Industrial Chemistry with a minimum acceptable UTME score of 180 [1]. FUNAAB’s official postgraduate-programmes page lists Industrial Chemistry among M.Sc./Ph.D. chemistry fields [4]. The Chemistry departmental page describes Chemistry generally and does not publish a separate Industrial Chemistry undergraduate structure [3]. Accordingly, the catalogue should not claim specific industrial chemistry units, laboratory sequences, or specialization options without a verified undergraduate curriculum.

### Mathematics

The official Mathematics department page identifies the B.Sc. (Hons) programme and explicitly states that its curriculum is planned to support broad knowledge and specialization according to aptitude in Pure or Applied Mathematics [6]. Its listed goals cover rigorous reasoning, mathematical techniques for science and technology, research, service instruction, and applications in employment fields including teaching, finance, software development, and research [6]. These are broad programme aims, not evidence that every named employment field is a formal specialization or that any particular course is offered.

### Physics

The official Physics page states that the department offers a single-honours Physics degree and describes its goal as producing skilled manpower for research and industrial applications [7]. It lists objectives concerning foundational knowledge, practical skills, applications, theoretical and practical problem solving, transferable skills, and preparation for further study [7]. No course-unit list should be generated from these objectives alone.

### Statistics

The official Statistics page describes a B.Sc. programme, its broad application range, objectives, and graduation requirements [8]. It states that students must complete required University, College/Department core, and elective courses and achieve a CGPA of at least 1.0; it also gives minimum duration rules by entry point [8]. These graduation rules do not identify individual course units, credits, or semester placement.

## Gaps requiring follow-up

1. No current, publicly indexed official undergraduate course handbook or complete semester-by-semester curriculum PDF was located for Chemistry, Geophysics, Industrial Chemistry, Mathematics, Physics, or Statistics.
2. Geophysics appears in the current admission list but not in the COLPHYS department list reviewed. Its administrative home and current undergraduate programme description require confirmation from the University or admission office.
3. Industrial Chemistry appears in the current admission list and postgraduate list, but no distinct public undergraduate department/programme page was located.
4. The report does not claim individual course units, credit loads, practical components, assessment methods, or specialization pathways unless the official page explicitly stated them.
5. The admission page gives minimum UTME scores, but those thresholds are not programme learning outcomes and may change by admission cycle.
