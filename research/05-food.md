# FUNAAB Academic Domain Research: Food Science and Human Ecology

**Scope.** This report covers the five undergraduate programmes listed by the Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta (FUNAAB) under the admission category **Food Science and Human Ecology**: Clothing & Textile Design; Food Science and Technology; Home Science and Management; Hospitality and Tourism; and Nutrition and Dietetics. It prioritizes official FUNAAB, college, departmental, admission, course-list, course-synopsis, and public PDF sources. It distinguishes programme-level descriptions from individual course-unit syllabi.

## Overall finding

FUNAAB’s official college page identifies four academic departments in the College of Food Science and Human Ecology (COLFHEC): Food Science and Technology, Home Science and Management, Hospitality and Tourism, and Nutrition and Dietetics.[1] The current 2026/2027 official admission page lists five programmes in this domain, including Clothing & Textile Design as a distinct admission choice.[2] The university’s public pages provide strong programme-level descriptions for Food Science and Technology, Home Science and Management, Hospitality and Tourism, and Nutrition and Dietetics. Clothing & Textile Design is documented principally as an option/subject area within Home Science and Management, while the current admission page lists it separately; no standalone undergraduate department page or complete programme handbook was located in the reviewed official material. Accordingly, the Clothing & Textile Design synopsis is **medium confidence**, while the other four are **high confidence**.

## Detailed programme notes

### 1. Clothing & Textile Design

The current FUNAAB admissions page lists **Clothing & Textile Design** as its own programme under Food Science and Human Ecology, with a minimum acceptable UTME score of 180 in the 2026/2027 listing.[2] The official Home Science and Management page, however, describes Clothing and Textiles as one of the department’s three current broad disciplines/options: Clothing, Textile and Interior Decoration; Child Development and Family Studies; and Home Science Extension.[3] Its programme philosophy states that Home Science and Management uses broad-based instruction, laboratory practicals, field demonstrations, workshops, and industrial training, and applies that instruction across the three disciplines.[3]

For catalogue purposes, the defensible synopsis is therefore an applied clothing/textile design and production pathway. The official HSM course list names **Introduction to Textiles and Clothing Design, Introduction to Clothing Techniques, Clothing Construction, Textile Production, Nigerian Clothing and Textile Industries, Pattern Drafting and Design, Principles and Technology of Textile Design and Production, Creative Fabrics, Advanced Construction and Tailoring Techniques, Applied Clothing Design, Design Consultancy, and Fashion Drawing**.[4] The same official page lists career areas including dressmaking and tailoring, clothing/textile production, weaving, pattern making, fabric design, embroidery, fashion design, merchandising, accessories, computer-aided design, and teaching/research.[3] These are supporting indicators of scope, not proof that every named unit is part of the separately listed current degree.

**Boundary.** The course titles and the HSM page should not be converted into a claim that the separate Clothing & Textile Design degree has exactly this curriculum. No standalone official undergraduate programme specification or complete current curriculum PDF was found in the reviewed sources.

### 2. Food Science and Technology

FUNAAB states that the department was established in 1983 and now offers bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees in various areas of Food Science and Technology.[5] Its official philosophy places the field at the intersection of mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, engineering applications, food raw-material properties and composition, storage, preservation, processing, and equipment-related principles.[5] The stated training model is multidisciplinary, giving students broad knowledge across Food Science and Technology before possible specialization.[5]

The department’s stated objectives include training food scientists and food-industry professionals, researching locally and globally relevant food problems, encouraging interdisciplinary cooperation, and preparing leaders for agro-industry, government, and food-security work.[5] The official course list includes units such as Introduction to Food Technology, Food Biochemistry, Food Microbiology, Food Quality Control and Plant Sanitation, Food Product Development, Food Packaging, Process Control and Automation, Food Plant Design and Pilot Demonstration, Cereals and Tuber Technology, Vegetable Technology, Fermented Foods, and Business Management for Food Technology.[6]

The official course-synopsis page provides unit-level descriptions. For example, FST 201 covers the food science/technology interface, food processing, preservation, storage, engineering units, agriculture, nutrition, and the roles of food technologists; FST 304 covers food microorganisms, foodborne disease, laboratory assessment, standards, and sanitary quality.[7] These detailed descriptions are **course-unit syllabi/synopses**, not a replacement for a programme-level curriculum map.

### 3. Home Science and Management

FUNAAB reports that Home Science and Management was established in 1988. It originally offered options including Clothing and Textile, Nutrition and Dietetics, Child Development and Family Studies, Home Furnishing and Interior Decoration, and Home Science Extension. After establishment of the Nutrition and Dietetics Department, the page identifies three current options: Clothing, Textile and Interior Decoration; Child Development and Family Studies; and Home Science Extension.[3]

The official philosophy defines Home Science and Management as the study of ways to maximize family quality of life through optimum use of human and material resources.[3] The page describes broad instruction in child development, family dynamics, textiles and clothing, extension, and household/institutional resource management, delivered through instruction, laboratory practicals, field demonstrations, workshops, and industrial training.[3] It states that graduates can specialize in the three disciplines/options and pursue productive or economic ventures supporting family wellbeing.[3]

The official course list corroborates the breadth of the programme with units in basic design, textiles and clothing design, child care and human development, housing, household resource management, clothing construction, extension programme development, home furnishing and housing design, interior decoration, pattern drafting, textile design and production, family/community counselling, institutional equipment and management, and fashion drawing.[4] Again, these are course titles and should not be presented as a current full degree structure without a current curriculum document.

### 4. Hospitality and Tourism

The official department page identifies the programme as established by Senate in January 2009, with first intake planned for October 2010. It says the programme was designed to complement Food Science and Technology, Home Science and Management, and Nutrition and Dietetics.[8] The page also reports full NUC accreditation for the Foodservice and Tourism programme and explains that the programme integrates the training needs of the catering and tourism industries.[8]

FUNAAB’s official justification connects the programme to catering, hotels, ready-to-eat and takeaway foods, changing consumer choice, food safety, packaging and labelling, and historical, traditional, modern, leisure, and entertainment tourism.[8] The stated interdisciplinary focus includes catering and hotel/hospitality management; food composition, preparation and preservation; food safety and quality assurance; marketing and consumer education; tourism development; tourism transportation services; and tourism laws and tourist management.[8]

The official philosophy emphasizes graduates who can operate in a dynamic business environment and who possess entrepreneurial, communication, presentation, teamwork, technology, and critical-thinking competence.[8] Objectives include human-resource preparation for catering, hotel, and tourism sectors; leadership and entrepreneurship; cost-effective management of human and material resources; self-employment and employment creation; research; and preparation for higher degrees.[8]

### 5. Nutrition and Dietetics

FUNAAB states that the Nutrition and Dietetics Department was established in October 2004 from the Nutrition and Dietetics option of Home Science and Management. It now offers bachelor’s, master’s, doctoral, and postgraduate-diploma programmes.[9] Its philosophy combines scientific and technological principles in human nutrition and dietetics and uses instruction, laboratory practicals, field demonstrations, and workshop practice to develop skilled graduates.[9]

The department’s mandate covers teaching and research in all aspects of Human Nutrition and Dietetics. Its objectives include preparing graduates for health, industrial, government, foodservice, community, and national settings; preventing and managing nutritional diseases; setting appropriate diets for healthy people and people with illness; building skills for higher study; teaching nutrient fundamentals and nutrition problems; and applying practical approaches to community nutrition.[9]

The official NTD course synopsis provides detailed unit descriptions, including Introduction to Human Nutrition and Dietetics, Community Nutrition, Public Health Nutrition, Human Nutrition, Nutrition in Disease, Clinical Nutrition, Diet Therapy I and II, Assessment of Nutritional Status, Nutrition Education, Institutional Food Production and Management, Nutrition Planning and Food Economics, and a supervised project.[10] The official course list is represented in the search-indexed university material by units such as recipe development and evaluation, community nutrition, and assessment of nutritional status.[11] The university also hosts a public NTD 313 Diet Therapy I PDF; it is a **course-unit document**, not a programme handbook.[12]

## Evidence limitations and interpretation rules

The official pages use changing labels across time, including “Hospitality and Tourism,” “Foodservice and Tourism,” and “Department of Hospitality and Tourism Management.” The report treats these as the same FUNAAB programme family only where the university’s own page provides the linkage.[8]

The official Home Science and Management page documents Clothing and Textiles as an option/discipline, while the 2026 admissions page lists Clothing & Textile Design separately.[2][3] This mismatch is the main reason for the medium confidence rating for Clothing & Textile Design. A tutor catalogue should preserve the admission-page name and avoid asserting a separate department, degree duration, credit total, or course sequence until FUNAAB publishes a current standalone specification.

The course lists and course-synopsis pages are useful for describing subject matter at unit level. They do not establish that every listed unit is compulsory, current, or part of the separately named Clothing & Textile Design admission programme. No course content was invented beyond what the cited official pages state.
