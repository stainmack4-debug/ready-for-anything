# FUNAAB Academic Domain Research: Veterinary Medicine

**Scope.** This report covers the listed FUNAAB programme **Veterinary Medicine**. FUNAAB’s official pages identify the undergraduate award as the **six-year Doctor of Veterinary Medicine (DVM)**. The report treats the DVM as the programme-level object. It does not treat individual course-unit synopses as separate programmes. Postgraduate Master of Veterinary Science and PhD offerings are recorded as out of scope.

## Catalogue synopsis

FUNAAB’s Veterinary Medicine programme is a six-year professional Doctor of Veterinary Medicine degree. Students admitted at 100 level undertake 12 semesters: a two-semester pre-professional phase followed by a ten-semester professional phase from 200 to 600 level. The professional phase is organized into three semesters of pre-clinical study, three semesters of para-clinical study, and four semesters of clinical study. The College states that the programme prepares students for animal health, economically important livestock production, public health, research, teaching, and veterinary practice, with an emphasis on producing veterinarians who can create employment. The professional curriculum is jointly delivered by the College’s veterinary departments, and all professional-phase courses are compulsory for the DVM award. Students must also complete at least six months of approved field study under veterinary supervision and submit a field-study report. Successful candidates receive the unclassified degree **Doctor of Veterinary Medicine (DVM)**. [1]

The published curriculum framework names, but does not by itself provide full unit-level outcomes for, the following areas: pre-clinical Veterinary Anatomy, Veterinary Biochemistry, Veterinary Physiology, and Animal Management; para-clinical Veterinary Pharmacology, Veterinary Parasitology, Veterinary Microbiology, and Veterinary Pathology; and clinical Veterinary Medicine, Veterinary Surgery, Veterinary Public Health, Theriogenology, Project, Clinics, and Field Study. [1] The official Veterinary Medicine and Surgery synopsis gives examples of individual course units, including introduction to veterinary medicine, general medicine, food-animal medicine, avian medicine, companion and laboratory-animal medicine, wildlife and fisheries medicine, medicine clinics, general principles of surgery, anaesthesia and intensive care, soft-tissue surgery and lameness, orthopaedic surgery and diagnostic imaging, and basic surgical exercises. Those entries are **course-unit syllabi/synopses**, not a replacement for the programme-level description. [3]

## Academic organization and learning coverage

The College’s official overview describes a broad professional purpose: animal health, livestock production, public health, research, teaching, and employment creation. [1] Its “At a Glance” page confirms that a Veterinary Teaching Hospital supports the clinical aspects of training and provides specialized clinical and diagnostic services to the local community. [2]

The official department page for Veterinary Anatomy describes anatomy as a pre-clinical foundation concerned with the form, disposition, and structure of tissues and organs in domestic animals. It specifically identifies gross anatomy, histology, and embryology as teaching and research fields and explains that anatomy supports later study in physiology, pathology, surgery, gynecology, medicine, and meat science. This is useful corroboration of the programme’s pre-clinical foundation, but it is not a complete DVM curriculum. [5]

The official course-synopsis page provides detailed descriptions for selected individual units. For example, VCM 102 introduces the meaning, history, education, accreditation, ethics, oath, regulation, organization, and opportunities of veterinary medicine. VCM 401 covers clinical history taking, environmental assessment, examination, diagnostic methods, sample handling, diagnosis, prognosis, and case management. VCM 501 and VCM 502 address diseases of economically important food animals and domestic birds, respectively. The listed surgery units address surgical principles, anaesthesia, intensive care, soft-tissue and orthopaedic surgery, lameness, diagnostic imaging, and practical surgical exercises. These statements should be used only when an AI tutor is answering at course-unit level. They should not be generalized into an exhaustive list of all DVM content. [3]

## Confidence and gaps

**Confidence: High** for the programme identity, DVM award, minimum duration, phase structure, compulsory professional-course rule, six-month field-study requirement, broad objectives, and 2026 entry requirements. These claims are supported by current or official FUNAAB pages and the official admission PDF.

**Gaps.** FUNAAB does not expose, in the reviewed public sources, a complete current course catalogue with every course code, semester, credit unit, prerequisite, learning outcome, assessment method, and clinical-rotation requirement. The course-synopsis page is selective and contains individual unit descriptions rather than a complete curriculum handbook. The official College overview also shows inconsistent department counts and labels across pages: one version lists more granular departments, while the “At a Glance” page presents six grouped departments. This does not change the verified programme-level structure, but department names should not be used as a proxy for a complete curriculum. Admission requirements can change by cycle; the linked 2026 PDF should be checked before operational use. Postgraduate specializations shown on the Postgraduate School page are not part of the undergraduate Veterinary Medicine programme record.
