# FUNAAB Academic Domain: Biological Sciences and Public Health

**Research scope.** This catalogue covers exactly the seven programmes listed by FUNAAB’s 2026/2027 pre-admission portal under Biological Science: Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Microbiology, Public Health, Pure and Applied Botany, Pure and Applied Zoology, and Science Lab Technology (the portal’s label for Science Laboratory Technology) [1]. The evidence hierarchy prioritises FUNAAB admission, college, departmental, and courseware pages, followed by official FUNAAB PDFs. Programme-level descriptions are kept separate from course-unit evidence. Where FUNAAB has announced a programme but has not published a substantive departmental curriculum page, that limitation is stated rather than inferred.

## Programme-level versus course-unit evidence

FUNAAB’s OpenCourseware index is useful corroboration that teaching resources exist in several established departments. It lists BCH units such as General Biochemistry, Biochemical Methods, Industrial Biochemistry, Enzymology, Plant Biochemistry, and Recombinant DNA; MCB units such as General Microbiology, Bacteriology, Immunology, Food Microbiology, Virology, Microbial Genetics, and Environmental Microbiology; and biological-science units such as Genetics, Ecology, Plant Pathology, Parasitology, Comparative Animal Physiology, and Animal Behaviour [11]. These entries are **course-unit evidence only**. They do not establish that every listed unit is compulsory, current, offered in a particular semester, or included in the seven programmes’ current approved curriculum. The separate Food Microbiology PDF is likewise a course document rather than a programme handbook [9].

## Research assessment

The strongest official evidence is available for **Microbiology, Pure and Applied Botany, Pure and Applied Zoology, and Biochemistry**, because FUNAAB publishes departmental programme descriptions. **Biotechnology, Public Health, and Science Laboratory Technology** are officially listed and announced as newly approved programmes, but the public record reviewed here does not yet expose their substantive undergraduate curricula. The report therefore intentionally uses conservative synopses for those three programmes and does not transfer content from similarly named postgraduate, veterinary, or individual course materials.
